## 1. Context for the reviewer

**The series.** *UptimeZ Inc.* is an office sitcom about a small AI company whose product, UptimeZ Find, always returns 0 RESULTS because no customer data was ever connected.

**Every episode:** one comic mechanism born of two competing desires; the first joke is physical and lands by 0:01; the story advances while the image loops; status shifts in every scene; corporate words stay in the background and the mechanisms are physical.

**The cast in this episode**
- **Don**, the CEO, sells for real and believes his own pitch. He **approves instead of investigating**. He has never made his own coffee: he lifts his cup and Pam fills it.
- **Father**, the investor and Don's father, is a calm nightclub kingpin who is never funny on purpose. Don's gold CEO throne, **$49,999** on its tag, was bought with Father's money.
- **Pam** can't stop fixing things and loves Don, who doesn't love her back. Her weapon is strategic silence.
- **Sofía** wants authority that doesn't depend on Don.
- **The Client** owns ten garages. He is a mechanic who works with his hands, and he is not an idiot.
- **The robot** (desk robot → waiter → HR) is technically true in every statement. It acts alone only within its permissions and **needs human approval** for money, access, commitments and system changes.

**Civil War so far.** Power is the gold throne, and the two camps are divided by a tape line on the office floor. E15 ended with Father hanging a card on the throne: **REVIEW · TOMORROW**. E16 is that review. E17, *The Big Bang*, ends the arc: the coffee machine explodes (caused by Don's unread "Approved." and an $80 repair bill nobody paid), and the Client is taken for dead but survives. Why he survives is planted here.

## 2. Premise

Father holds a review to choose who will run the company. What he is really looking for is the son he wished he had. He has almost chosen the Client, a man with good hands, when the robot he rejected comes back with the report nobody let it present, and with the one bill nobody knew existed. Father begins to doubt. The robot sits in the throne, and the handover needs one approval. Father tries to give it and can't: the approver is the CEO. Don reads what he is approving for the first time in his life, understands it, and deliberately says nothing.

**The engine.** The same words appear twice, **HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED**, in the same type, with the same hand gesture:
1. At the coffee machine, Don slaps the button without looking and approves danger.
2. At the throne, Don's hand rises to the same button, hovers, freezes, pulls back. He withholds something good to keep his chair.

The mechanism is the same and the outcome is opposite. Every beat builds toward that silence.

## 3. Beat sheet (timing lock 64 s; the animatic may stretch it toward the bible's 90 s, but the 2 s silence is fixed)

**Frame 0 / the seam (0–4 s) · close shot: Don, throne, card, the robot's hand**
Don is asleep in the throne, tilted back with his weight on it. A folded card wedges the front caster. The robot's hand pulls the card out. The throne rolls back and Don slides off onto the floor. *(The close shot keeps the robot's badge out of frame: it still has it at 10 s, but not at the loop's end.)*
Flat on his back, eyes still shut, he raises his empty cup straight up like a baby bird, and shakes it twice:
**DON:** "Pam! Coffee."
Wider: across the tape Pam, holding the Client's MY GARAGES blueprint, looks at the cup and turns to the next page. Her mustard mug sits forgotten on the counter by the coffee machine.

**The review (4–8 s)**
Father unfolds a cheap folding chair beside the gold throne.
**FATHER:** "Executive."
It wobbles. Without being asked, the Client kneels, takes a washer from his pocket and fixes it. Father sits and leans hard to one side; the chair doesn't budge. His eyes go to Don's gold throne, then to the Client. Card: REVIEW · TODAY.

**Sofía (8–10 s)**
Sofía sits in the throne, chin up, legs crossed. Father looks past her before she has finished sitting. She finishes anyway, gets up, and her eyes are already on the robot.

**The robot (10–16 s)**
The robot rolls up holding out a report. Father raises a hand before it can present it. The robot slowly retracts the report. Its screen shows **POSITION VACANT**, because it left HR to apply.
Sofía's eyes snap to the screen and she pivots on the spot: she unpins its HR badge, pins it on herself, checks that it hangs straight, and only then points the robot to the corner. Her first act in HR is dismissing the last HR. *(Close shot on the badge.)*
One oil-drop tear, then the screen goes flat: **NOTED.** It rolls to the corner, report still in hand.

**Don's coffee (16–29 s)**
Don will prove he doesn't need Pam: he makes the coffee himself, for the first time.
1. He puts the filter in upside down, tries to hand it to the Client, sees Father watching, and takes it back.
2. The panel reads **HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED.** Don slaps the button without looking: **DON:** "Approved." *(This is gesture 1, and the button is visible.)*
   The machine shakes and starts creeping toward the counter edge, and the needle starts to climb. A small **tick** from the machine: only the Client's head turns, a beat before anything happens. Then a visible jet of steam with a hiss. The Client is already dropping flat under the counter, eyes shut, and without looking catches Pam's mug as it slides off the edge. *(A professional's instinct, not fright: he heard it first.)*
3. The coffee drops into the cup in one tar-like glug. Don plants a spoon in it and the spoon stands upright. He tilts it to one side; it stays diagonal. Pleased, he sets the cup by Father. Hold: Father doesn't touch it.

**Almost decided (29–36 s)**
Father props the Client's name card on the throne. Don, petty, nudges it crooked with one finger. Father straightens it without looking, then leans to the Client under the counter, who still holds the mug. A barely visible smile, for the Client only:
**FATHER:** "Good hands."
Don sees the smile. He looks down at his own hands.
**PAM** (her eyes going from Father to the robot in the corner, the refused report in its hand): "Did anyone review the business?"

**The robot returns (36–47 s)**
From the corner:
**ROBOT:** "Yes."
It rolls up and the report unfurls from its chest, accordion-folding onto the floor over Don's shoes. Don steps back; the paper follows. Another step, more paper. The documentation physically pushes the CEO out.
From the report the robot tears off one sheet and hands it to Father: the **coffee-machine repair invoice, $80, UNPAID**, the bill nobody in the company knew existed. Father reads it, the only person reading anything, and pockets it.
1. Screen: **UPTIME 100%** → Don's eyes close mid-yawn, his head nods, he jerks awake. Hold.
2. Screen: **SALARY $0** → Father straightens in his cheap chair → his eye drops to the **$49,999** tag under the throne's armrest → then to Don, smiling proudly, who thinks Father is admiring the chair. Hold. *(Father, too, is moved by the wrong number.)*
Father takes the Client's card off the throne: the card in one hand, the report in the other. Doubt.
*(In the background the needle keeps climbing. Nobody looks.)*

**Human approval required (47–60 s)**
The robot sits in the throne. Its screen reads **HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED**, in the same words and type as the machine.
Father reaches to approve it himself. The screen changes: **APPROVER: CEO**, with Don's face. Father's finger stops. He turns to Don. Everyone turns to Don. *(The CEO seat can be handed over only by the CEO. Father owns the money, but not the chair.)*
Push in on Don's eyes: for the first time he reads what he is approving. His hand rises toward the button, the same gesture as at the machine. It hovers. He sees the robot in his throne. The hand freezes, pulls back, and he folds his arms. *(Gesture 2.)*
**Two full seconds of silence.** Sofía adjusts her new HR badge.
The robot sits dead still a beat, then rises slowly and stiffly out of the throne, formal to the last.
Father flips the card to **REVIEW · TOMORROW**, folds his chair and leaves with the report, the Client's card and the $80 invoice in his pocket.

**Loop (60–64 s) · the same close shot as frame 0**
Don folds REVIEW · TOMORROW the same way as in frame 0 and wedges it under the front caster. He sits, leans back with his weight on the throne, folds his arms and falls asleep. In the dark the robot's screen glows **UPTIME 100%**. Its hand reaches for the card → frame 0.
*(On a second viewing, the card under the caster was REVIEW · TOMORROW all along.)*

## 5. What E16 plants for E17

- The needle has climbed into the red, and the machine sits at the counter edge.
- **The $80 repair invoice is in Father's pocket, still unpaid.** The machine was never repaired.
- The Client hears a machine go wrong before anyone else, and drops flat. That is why he survives the explosion.
- Father has two candidates and has taken both proofs with him.
- The robot is openly after the throne. Don holds it only by withholding "Approved."; the CEO seat can be handed over only by the CEO.

E16 stands alone: the main question is who runs the company. The machine is the second threat.

## 6. Closed decisions (please don't re-propose)

- **Title:** "Approved." (*Human Approval Required* rejected as interface jargon that dates fast and gives the callback away; *The Review* was the working title.)
- **Pam has one line** plus her silent rebellion (the blueprint page). Her earlier closing aphorism is cut.
- **The robot's oil-drop tear and the HR badge passing to Sofía** stay; the tear is followed by NOTED. and triggers its return.
- **Mom** is not in this episode.
- **Father's folding chair and "Executive."** stay. No ✓/X cards: Father judges by not looking.
- **Don's ending** is a deliberate silence (hand hovers, pulls back), not a habitual "Approved." He keeps the throne into E17.
- **The coffee jokes** come from Don's helplessness without Pam, not from the machine. He says "Approved." at the machine once.
- **Parked for E17:** the sinking throne, the elbow chin-slip, Father pushing an item back with one finger.
