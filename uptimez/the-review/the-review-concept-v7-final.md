# UptimeZ Inc. — E16 "Approved." · Final concept (v7, for review)

**Format:** 64-second vertical (9:16) animated short, paper cut-out style · **Series arc:** Civil War, part 4 of 5 · **Must work on mute** · Loops seamlessly

---

## 1. Context for the reviewer

**The series.** *UptimeZ Inc.* is an office sitcom about a small AI company. Its product, UptimeZ Find, always returns 0 RESULTS, because no customer data was ever connected.

**Rules every episode follows.** One comic mechanism built from two competing desires. The first joke is physical and lands by 0:01. The story advances while the image loops. Status shifts in every scene. Corporate words stay in the background, and the mechanisms are physical.

**The cast in this episode**
- **Don**, the CEO, sells for real and believes his own pitch. He **approves instead of investigating**. He has never once made his own coffee: he lifts his cup and Pam fills it.
- **Father** is the investor and Don's father: a calm nightclub kingpin, never funny on purpose. Don's gold CEO throne, **$49,999** on its tag, was bought with Father's money.
- **Pam** can't stop fixing things and loves Don without being loved back. Her weapon is strategic silence.
- **Sofía** wants authority independent of Don.
- **The Client** owns ten garages: a mechanic, a man who works with his hands, and not an idiot.
- **The robot** (desk robot → waiter → HR) is technically true in every statement. It acts alone only within its permissions and **needs human approval** for money, access, commitments and system changes.

**Civil War so far.** Power is the gold throne, and the two camps are divided by a tape line on the office floor. E15 ended with Father hanging a card on the throne: **REVIEW · TOMORROW**. E16 is that review. E17, *The Big Bang*, ends the arc: the coffee machine explodes, the Client is taken for dead but survives, and the reason he survives has to be planted here.

---

## 2. Premise

Father holds a review to choose who will run the company, but what he is really looking for is the son he wished he had. He has almost chosen the Client, a man with good hands, when the robot he rejected comes back with the report nobody let it present. Father begins to doubt. The robot sits in the throne, and its screen asks for one human's approval. Don, the only person who can give it, reads what he is approving for the first time in his life, understands it, and deliberately says nothing.

**The engine.** The same words appear twice, **HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED**, in the same type:
1. At the coffee machine, Don approves something dangerous without reading it.
2. In the throne, Don reads it, understands, and **withholds** something good to keep his chair.

The mechanism is the same both times and the outcome is the opposite. Every beat builds toward that silence.

## 3. Beat sheet

**Frame 0 / the seam (0–4 s)**
Don is asleep in the throne, tilted back with his weight on it. A folded card wedges the front caster. The robot pulls the card out, the throne rolls back, and Don slides off onto the floor. Flat on his back, eyes still shut, he raises his empty cup straight up like a baby bird:
**DON:** "Pam! Coffee."
Across the tape, Pam is holding the Client's MY GARAGES blueprint. She looks at the cup and turns to the next page. Her mustard mug sits forgotten on the counter by the coffee machine.

**The review (4–8 s)**
Father unfolds a cheap folding chair beside the gold throne.
**FATHER:** "Executive."
The chair wobbles. Without being asked, the Client kneels, takes a washer from his pocket and fixes it. Father sits and tests it: solid. He glances at the Client. Card: REVIEW · TODAY.

**Sofía (8–11 s)**
Sofía sits in the throne, chin up, legs crossed. Father looks past her before she has finished sitting. She finishes anyway, then gets up.

**The robot (11–17 s)**
The robot rolls up holding out a report. Father raises a hand before it can present it. The robot slowly retracts the report. Its screen shows **POSITION VACANT**: it left HR to apply. Sofía unpins its HR badge, pins it on herself and points the robot to the corner. Her first act in HR is dismissing the last HR. One oil-drop tear, then the screen goes flat: **NOTED.** It rolls to the corner.

**Don's coffee (17–29 s)**
Don will prove he doesn't need Pam by making the coffee himself, for the first time.
1. He puts the filter in upside down. He tries to hand the filter to the Client, sees Father watching, and takes it back.
2. The panel reads **HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED.** **DON** (not looking): "Approved."
   The machine shakes and starts creeping toward the edge of the counter, and the needle starts to climb. When it hisses, the Client drops flat under the counter by reflex, eyes shut. Without looking, he catches Pam's mug as it slides off the edge.
3. The coffee drops into the cup in one tar-like glug. Don plants a spoon in it, and the spoon stays upright. He sets the cup by Father. Hold: Father doesn't touch it.

**Almost decided (29–36 s)**
Father props the Client's name card on the throne. Don, petty, nudges it crooked with one finger. Father straightens it without looking, then leans down to the Client under the counter, who still has the mug in his hand:
**FATHER:** "Good hands."
Don looks down at his own hands.
**PAM** (to Father): "Did anyone review the business?"

**The robot returns (36–50 s)**
From the corner:
**ROBOT:** "Yes."
It rolls up, and the report it was refused unfurls from its chest, accordion-folding onto the floor over Don's shoes. Don shuffles back a step. Father reads it, the only person reading anything.
1. Screen: **UPTIME 100%** → Don's eyes close mid-yawn, his head nods and he jerks awake. Hold.
2. Screen: **SALARY $0** → Father straightens in his cheap chair → his eye drops to the **$49,999** tag hanging under the throne's armrest → then to Don, smiling proudly, who thinks Father is admiring the chair. Hold.
Father takes the Client's card off the throne and holds the card in one hand and the report in the other. Doubt.
*(In the background the needle keeps climbing. Nobody looks at it.)*

**Human approval required (50–60 s)**
The robot sits in the throne. Its screen reads **HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED**, the same words and type as the machine. Everyone turns to Don.
Push in on his eyes: for the first time, he reads what he is approving. His mouth opens for "Approved." He sees the robot in his throne. He closes his mouth and folds his arms.
**Two full seconds of silence.** Sofía adjusts her new HR badge.
The robot sits dead still for a beat, then rises slowly and stiffly out of the throne, formal to the last.
Father flips the card to **REVIEW · TOMORROW**, folds his chair and leaves with both the report and the Client's card.

**Loop (60–64 s)**
Don folds REVIEW · TOMORROW the same way as in frame 0 and wedges it under the front caster. He sits, leans back with his weight on the throne, folds his arms and falls asleep. In the dark, the robot's screen glows **UPTIME 100%**. It reaches for the card → frame 0.
*(On a second viewing, the card under the caster was REVIEW · TOMORROW all along.)*

## 5. What E16 plants for E17

- The needle has climbed into the red, and the coffee machine is at the edge of the counter.
- The Client's reflex of dropping flat at a hiss is established. It is why he survives the explosion.
- Father has two candidates and has taken both proofs with him.
- The robot is openly after the throne. Don keeps it only by withholding "Approved."

E16 also stands on its own. The main question is who runs the company; the machine is the secondary threat.

---

## 6. Closed decisions (please don't re-propose)

- **Title:** "Approved." *Human Approval Required* was rejected as interface jargon that will date fast and give the callback away. *The Review* was the working title.
- **Pam gets one line**, plus her silent rebellion (turning the blueprint page). Her earlier closing aphorism has been cut.
- **The robot's oil-drop tear and the HR badge passing to Sofía** stay. The tear is followed by NOTED. and triggers the robot's return.
- **Mom** is not in this episode.
- **Father's folding chair and "Executive."** stay. There are no ✓/X cards; Father judges by not looking.
- **Don's ending:** a deliberate silence, not a habitual "Approved." He keeps the throne going into E17.
- **The coffee jokes** come from Don's helplessness without Pam, not from the machine. He says "Approved." at the machine only once.
- **Parked for E17:** the sinking throne, the elbow chin-slip, and Father pushing an item back with one finger.

## 7. Questions for this review

1. Does the chain build **vertically**, with each beat making the next one more uncomfortable, all the way to Don's silence?
2. **Mute test:** with no sound and no reading of small text, which beats fail?
3. Is 64 s honest for this many beats? Where would you take 2 s back to give the silence more room?
4. Does Father's motive (looking for the son he wished he had) read from behaviour alone: the washer, the catch, "Good hands.", Don looking at his hands?
5. Is Don's silence funny **and** dark: his first intelligent decision and his most selfish one?
