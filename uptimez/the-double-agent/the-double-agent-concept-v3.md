## 1. In one line

**Father's meeting without Don takes place inside Don's own office, and Don, sure he has a spy in there, opens his own border to let her back in.**

**The principle (from the review, and Don's private lie in STORYLINE):** Don never loses because he is stupid. He loses because he is sure he is winning. Every beat answers one question: **how does Don's attempt to regain power reduce his power?**

## 3. The mechanism and escalation

**Everything Don does to keep control helps Pam serve the other side.** Don loses physical authority (Act I), then organizational authority (Act II), then hands over authority himself while believing he has taken it back (Act III).

| Crossing | Meaning | Pam | What Don does that makes it possible |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **An accident** | Brings coffee; the Client takes her in with it | He ordered the coffee |
| 2 | **A choice** | Takes her notepad back in, deliberately | He is admiring himself in the glass |
| 3 | **Authorized** | Ducks under the tape back to the meeting | He lifts the tape for her |

Mom is Pam's accomplice inside the A-story: her shoulder pads hide crossing 1, and her step off the tape (0:25) keeps Don from walking in on the truth.

## 5. The tape

Tomas's condition from v2 still holds: convincing, funny, fun. Two jokes plus the loop, and the meaning escalates: **the border traps its maker → the maker opens his border for the other side's agent.**

### 5.1 The opener: cause, effect, consequence

| Time | Event | What must read | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00.0 | **Cause:** frame 0, close and low: the leftover roll from 14 by the throne, its free end curled up, sticky side up, shiny. Don's shoe coming down | The tail is sticky. **Tested two ways** on stills: with a scrap stuck to it (a blue confetti strip from 9) and with a shine only. The simpler one that reads goes in | — |
| 0:00.4 | **Effect:** the shoe lands; he lifts his foot and the roll comes up with it | The roll follows the shoe (the joke by 0:01) | tacky *thk* |

| 0:03.2 | Don pulls the roll off his sole and drops it by the throne | It stays where it falls (no trick landing: the loop only needs the roll in that spot) | thud |

### 5.2 The payoff: the barrier (0:46–0:53)

- Don is at his line, on his own side. Pam beside him, on his side.
- **He crouches, picks at the tape's edge and peels a long strip up off the floor**, the sticky side catching dust, and holds it high in both hands **like a border barrier**, chin up, proud, the official of his own checkpoint.
- **Pam ducks under it** without a word and walks back into the meeting.
- He keeps holding it. Then (0:53) the salute: one hand to his brow, the other holding the strip like a flag.
- **Simple to draw:** two hands on one strip, no reach, no balance, no contact with another body (lesson 178: simplest gesture).
- **Readable on mute:** a raised barrier and someone ducking under it is a picture everyone knows.
- **Continuity:** the opener showed the tape peels. Where he peeled it, the line has a gap; in 16 the gap is still there (the border has a hole Don made himself).

### 5.3 The loop

Don lowers the strip and steps down from the throne toward the REVIEW tag; his shoe lands on the roll's sticky tail. **The loop's cut is the shoe-and-roll close-up**: the throne, the tags and the gap in the line are out of that frame, so the last drawing equals frame 0 while the story has moved on (lesson 183: a loop is a state; masked as in 14).

### 5.4 No tape elsewhere

0:25 is Mom's step (no wobble on the line). **Episode 16 opens on something else** (14 opened on the blinds, 15 on the tape). 16's opener candidate: the robot's badge (moved from 15).
