## The protect list (gate G1)

1. **Four refusals, each worse:** walked round (1.2), walked over (3.5), turned off (6.4), posted (7.2). Each is a body doing something; each is followed by Pam's face and a small action of hers.
2. **The hit-and-shrug, and Don wiping his bumper** and then straightening his vest. He checks the car, never her.
3. **The spotlight turned away** (6.4–6.5): the envelope held high, a pale glove turning the lamp to the cards, Pam a black shape against the lit room. The shot and the cover.
4. **The same arm, awake:** still up, and Sofía posts the rota into it. PAM on all seven lines.
5. **"Only for you":** big on the marquee in the dream; awake, said in passing to a woman who is asleep, about the coffee duty.

## G0, named

**The first joke, physical, by 0:01:** shot 1.2. A masked hero in a perfect pose over three flattened thugs; the woman she saved walks round her without looking up from her phone.

## Rules of staging

- **Her face carries every joke.** A narrow half mask and cat ears; eyes, brows and mouth readable at phone size (lesson 163). After each refusal: the pose holds, the pose sags, a small action (a take, lesson 21). The cut never comes on the gag's own frame (lesson 23).
- **Nobody ever looks at her,** in the dream or awake. Every eyeline goes past her, to a visible target: a phone, a bumper, a bill, a map, the cards, a mug.
- **The pale gloves are the tell.** The shadows are bare-headed black shapes with pale gloves and nothing else; no hats, so they are never taken for Office Noir 2's Shadow or for the Client (lesson 36).
- **No action under half a second** (lesson 158): the bump, the two taps, the lift from the coat, the glove on the lamp and the posting each get a pause before and time after.
- **Hands:** the series' one hand (`kit.HAND`), whole-hand gestures, each prop in the hand of the arm that holds it (lessons 147, 178–181). A flat palm on the bumper; a finger tapping; the envelope gripped at its edge.
- **Inserts have an owner** (lesson 134): the Client's sleeve and watch in 2.3; the velvet cuff and ring for Dad; a pale glove for a shadow; Sofía's sleeve in 7.2.
- **Pam in the road is a lying body,** drawn as one (lesson 153), not the standing figure laid down.
- **The dream is black and white,** built with the noir kit (`on_set.blinds`, `key_light`, `vignette`, a lit side and a dark side) in every location; faces lighter than what is behind them (lesson 50). Colour in the dream: the neon and the envelope's warm tone. Awake, colour.
- **Narration** at the top, under the bar (y 96), white italics with a dark edge (lessons 33, 34).
- **Dead zones (G3):** faces and story props out of y > 720 and off the right-hand rail; in the road shots the camera is low and the road line sits at about y 640.
- **Sizes:** the series' size for everyone (lesson 164), lined up on one floor line before the layouts (lesson 137).

## Voices and sound

- **Pam's narration:** her British voice by audition, lower and drier than her office voice. Three lines, each over a picture that undercuts it. It stops at the marquee.
- **Sofía:** `af_bella`, offhand, said in passing. In a bubble; the tail to her mouth as drawn in 7.2.
- **Line budget:** 4. No other line. Don's whistle, the two taps, the bump, the clink.
- **Music:** the jazz-noir cue of the first films through the dream, cut dead on 6.3 (the silence of her waiting is the joke, lesson 41). Room tone and the clink awake, kept low enough for the recognizer (lesson 105). Rain under 8.2 into the seam.

## For the cold viewers (G4)

**The five beats they must tell back:** (1) she saved the woman, and the woman walked past her; (2) she saw two men in a car with an envelope and followed; (3) a car hit her, and the cop checked the car, and the others walked over her for the money; (4) her name was on the sign, she stole the envelope and held it up, and they turned the light off her; (5) it was a dream: a colleague puts an envelope in her hand, it is the coffee rota with only her name, and she goes back to sleep.

**Direct questions, after the open prompt:**
1. Who is the cop, and what does he look at after the car hits her?
2. What do the people who step over her have in common with the ones lying in the street at the start?
3. What is written on the sign, and who says those words later?
4. What is in the envelope in the dream, and what is in it when she wakes?
5. Does the woman who gives her the envelope look at her?
6. The video loops: what does she do at the very end, and why?

## Open decisions for stop 1

1. **Pam's remark.** Her line has a rule: "Some [what the others do]. Others [what Pam does]." (lesson 165), and Office Noir 2 ended on one. This script has none. A candidate that keeps the rule, said in 8.1 before the mask comes down: **"Some shine. Others brew."** Or none, and the film ends silent.
2. **The map's Z** has to read at 270 px. If it doesn't, the map goes and the scheme is the envelope alone.
3. **Length.** If the takes run long, cut 4.2 (4.1 carries the sign), then 5.2.
