# Office Noir 4 — concept v2

UptimeZ Inc. · special · Pam's dream · for review · nothing is built from this yet

**v2 (23 Nov)** takes in the second outside review and Tomas's picks: the comedy becomes a ladder of three refusals (ignored hero → ignored victim → ignored star), Pam *tries* to be seen at every step, the dream's envelope wakes up as something ordinary (the Office Noir form), and she wakes at her desk, not in bed. v1 is kept next to this file. Kept from v1: the engine (Pam-Cat alone, unseen), Dad as a deliberate flash-forward, the pale-gloved shadows, the Client keeping the envelope, Don waving the car through, every line Tomas wrote.

**Working title:** *Office Noir: Seven Lives*. Others: *Office Noir: The Cat Who Wasn't There*, *Office Noir: Only for You*.

**Target length:** about 65 seconds, nine beats including frame 0.

## The idea in one line

Pam dreams she is the noir hero who sees everything, and does everything right, and keeps trying to be noticed for it. Nobody looks: not the woman she saves, not the cop who runs her over, not the casino that has her name in lights. She wakes at her desk, and the only thanks she gets is for coffee.

**Two wants that collide:** Pam-Cat wants to be seen solving it. The Client and Dad want their deal (ten garages folded into UptimeZ) to stay invisible. Their secrecy and her need to be seen are the same problem: she is the one person in the dream nobody looks at, so she is the only one who can catch them, and the only one who can't be thanked for it.

**One mechanism:** each beat she does something heroic and **poses for it**, and each beat the world looks somewhere else. Each refusal is worse than the last: walked past, walked over, turned off.

**One image:** the casino spotlight on Pam-Cat, the stolen envelope held up in triumph, and a pale-gloved hand turning the lamp away from her to the card table. She is left in the dark with her arm still up.

**Its twin, awake:** at her desk, the same arm up, holding an internal-mail envelope. Inside: the coffee rota, PAM on all seven lines.

## The protect list (for G1)

1. **Three refusals, each worse:** walked round (01), walked over (03), turned off (06). Each is a body doing something, and each is followed by half a second of Pam's face.
2. **The hit-and-shrug, and Don wiping his bumper.** She's fine before the line lands; he checks the car, never her.
3. **The spotlight turned away.** One spotlight, the envelope held high, a pale glove turning the lamp to the cards. This is the shot and the cover.
4. **The deflation.** The dream's envelope wakes up as an internal-mail envelope holding the coffee rota, PAM ×7: the same arm, the same hold, an ordinary thing (the Office Noir rule: every dream object has a real twin).
5. **"Only for you."** Set up as the marquee's advertising line and paid off as Sofía handing over a coffee: the first thanks Pam gets, for the wrong thing.

## How it looks and sounds

- **Picture:** black and white in the dream, as *Office Noir* and *Office Noir 2*, with one colour: the neon marquee. The envelope is the one warm tone in the dream, and the rota envelope the same tone awake, so the eye matches them. The office awake is in colour. The lighting kit from the earlier films (blinds, key light, vignette) runs through every dream location for unity.
- **The mask:** a half mask and ears only. Eyes, brows and mouth stay readable at phone size.
- **Narration:** Pam's voice, lower and drier than her usual one, three lines only, each over a picture that undercuts it: "I know how to survive" over her sagging pose; "seven lives" while she sits up in the road among the feet. The voice stops when she sees the marquee.
- **Music:** the jazz-noir cue in the dream, cut dead when the spotlight turns away (the silence is the joke, lesson 41). Office room tone, a mug on a desk, when she wakes.

## What it must not break

- **Never requited.** Don is a traffic cop who doesn't look at her; nothing romantic. The wake-up is Sofía.
- **Dad is a flash-forward, not a retcon.** The dream previews him before episode 12, as the first *Office Noir* previewed Mom before episode 8. He never says a line that only makes sense if he has already met the Client in the main timeline; the deal is shown (a map, a logo), never explained.
- **Sofía's CV is inside the dream only.** It echoes the plant of episodes 12 and 14 (her CV in a bodyguard's pocket). Awake, the envelope holds the coffee rota, so nothing prophetic crosses into the series.
- **Nothing is carried into the next numbered episode.** The status quo holds: Pam unseen, the coffee still hers to make.
- **Don't develop:** Pam-Cat is a costume on an existing character, not a new recurring one.

## Risks I can see now

| Risk | Why | What I would do |
|---|---|---|
| The shadows stepping over her reads as cruel | A woman lying in the road, people walking over her | Fast and cartoon: high steps, nobody looks down, one stops to pick a bill off her coat. Her straightened mask ends it before pity starts |
| The car hit reads as violent | A car hitting a person | Three frames (headlights, contact, already flat), no impact thud, Don's bumper wipe is the laugh |
| Five locations in 65 s | Street, car, crossing, casino, office | Street, car and crossing are one street; casino is one booth and one spotlight; the office is the existing desk |
| The marquee has too much text for a phone | Four phrases on one sign | PAM-CAT big; the three phrases small and stacked; test at 270 px (G3). If it fails, PAM-CAT and ONLY FOR YOU only |
| The rota must read in one glance | It is the deflation | One insert, the sheet filling the frame: COFFEE ROTA, seven rows, PAM in each |
| Sofía walking past reads as Sofía being cruel | She is on her phone, not snubbing | Her eyes never leave the phone; she doesn't see Pam at all, which is the point |
