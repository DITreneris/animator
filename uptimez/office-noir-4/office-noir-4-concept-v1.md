# Office Noir 4 — concept v1

UptimeZ Inc. · special · Pam's dream · for review · nothing is built from this yet

Written from Tomas's outline (23 Nov) and the decisions made in review: the engine is Pam-Cat alone, working unseen; the Client/Dad scheme is a deliberate flash-forward (as Mom was in the first *Office Noir*); the wake-up is Sofía in a robe with coffee, not Don in the bed.

**Working title:** *Office Noir: Seven Lives*. Two others to choose from: *Office Noir: The Cat Who Wasn't There*, *Office Noir: Seven Lives, One Envelope*.

**Target length:** about 60–70 seconds, eight beats.

## The idea in one line

Pam dreams she is the only one who ever sees anything — a cat burglar nobody notices — and proves it by single-handedly uncovering a scheme the whole office is blind to. She still wakes up unseen.

**Two wants that collide:** Pam-Cat wants to be seen as the one who solves it (the dream's version of her real want: Don's recognition). The Client and Dad want their deal — ten garages folded into UptimeZ — to stay invisible to everyone, including Sofía and Don.

**One mechanism:** every scene plays as a hard-boiled detective triumph — she survives a hit, cracks the case, pulls off the heist — but the picture keeps reminding us nobody in the scene is watching her do any of it. She is as invisible inside the fantasy as she is outside it.

**One image:** Pam-Cat under the casino's neon marquee, cat-eared mask, the stolen envelope held open to the light with Sofía's CV photo glowing inside it — and three feet behind her, the Client and Dad at their booth, backs turned, never once looking her way.

**Its twin, awake:** the same held-up envelope, now a real one in her hand at her own desk, with Sofía holding two coffees and asking "what's that?" — the dream object survives into the morning.

| **Sofía** | A woman walking alone at night, unaware she's being followed by shadows | To get home; has no idea she's in anyone's story but her own | Checks over her shoulder a beat too late, every time |
| **Don** | A traffic cop, silhouette only until the headlights catch his badge | To "do his job" — waves the Client's sedan through without ever looking her way | Keeps his stance and his signal arm moving, never turns his head |

## The eight beats

| # | Picture (what a viewer sees with the sound off) | Narration / lines |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **The street.** Night, one streetlamp. Sofía walks alone; pale-gloved shadows peel off the wall behind her, closing in. A cat-eared silhouette drops from above between her and them | N (Pam-Cat): "I know how to survive." |
| 02 | **The save.** Pam-Cat fights the shadows off in three quick beats (a trip, a shove, a clean exit), never showing her face to Sofía, who walks on not knowing she was ever in danger | N: "I know where to see what others do not see." |
| 03 | **The car.** A sedan idles at the curb. Inside: the Client and Dad. The Client taps an envelope twice against his knee — his tell — and keeps it in his own coat; Dad gets only a nod. Pam-Cat watches from a fire escape, unseen | (no line; the picture carries it) |
| 04 | **The crossing.** Pam-Cat crosses on foot, following the car. Don-as-cop, badge catching the light, waves the sedan straight through without turning his head. It clips her; she rolls, lands flat on her back, dazed stars — then sits up, straightens her mask, completely fine, and keeps walking | N: "I am like a cat. I have seven lives." |
| 05 | **The money.** The car window opens; Dad's hand throws a fold of bills onto the street. The same pale-gloved shadows from the alley scramble out of the gutter to collect it. The car pulls away. Pam-Cat, still sprawled, watches it all | N: "One evening. Once in a lifetime. Only for you." |
| 06 | **The casino.** Neon. A marquee poster: PAM-CAT, ON STAGE TONIGHT. Inside, the Client and Dad at a booth, a folded city map between them with ten boxes and a bigger logo over it. Around them, a shadow card game — the same pale gloves dealing, nobody's face shown | (no line; the room says it) |
| 07 | **The lift.** On stage, mid-act, Pam-Cat passes close enough to the booth to lift the envelope from the Client's own coat — the one he's kept on him since beat 03 — without either man looking up. She steps into the one spotlight, opens it | (no line; held on the reveal) |
| 08 | **The reveal / wake.** Inside the envelope: Sofía's CV, the same photograph from her welcome pack. Hard cut: Pam's own bedroom, dawn. Sofía stands over her bed in a robe with two coffee mugs, dazed Pam blinking, cat-ear pillow crease still on her cheek. In Pam's other hand, half under the blanket: a real envelope | SOFÍA: "Rough night?" PAM, still half-asleep: "...only for you." (she looks down, sees the envelope, goes quiet) |

**The loop:** the dream opens on Pam's dark bedroom and closes on the same framing, lit for morning — but her hand, empty at the start, now holds the envelope.

## The protect list (first draft, for G1)

1. **Pam-Cat is never seen by anyone she helps or exposes.** Sofía never learns who saved her; the Client and Dad never notice who lifted the envelope. The whole point is that she succeeds completely and still isn't seen.
2. **The hit-and-shrug.** Don's cop silhouette clips her; she's fine before the line lands. No injury beat, no pity — it's a flex, not a wound.
3. **The envelope survives the wake.** What's inside stays the same object from dream to morning: this is the one dream-to-reality twin the episode needs (per the Office Noir 1 rule), and it's also the plant.
4. **Sofía's coffee-mug entrance.** Two mugs, robe, "rough night?" — the real callback to ep1, not a new prop.
5. **The marquee image is the only "on stage" shot.** One spotlight, one envelope held to the light, the Client and Dad with their backs turned. Nothing else from the casino is a hero shot.

## How it looks and sounds

- Picture: black and white, as the first two *Office Noir* films; the casino gets one colour accent (the neon marquee), the same way *Office Noir 2* kept one red. The envelope and its CV photo stay the one warm colour, dream and awake both.
- Narration: Pam's voice, lower and drier than her usual register, same device as Sofía's narration in *Office Noir 2*.
- Music: jazz-noir cue through the dream; cuts clean on the wake, the way the bang cut to the cork in *Office Noir 2*.

## What it must not break

- **Never requited stays intact.** Don never appears as a love interest in this episode; the wake-up is Sofía, not Don. (This replaces Tomas's original sketch ending — flagged and changed in review, 23 Nov.)
- **Father's identity as investor stays a flash-forward, not a retcon.** This episode previews Dad and the garages-into-UptimeZ deal before episode 12, the same way the first *Office Noir* previewed Mom as real before episode 8. Nothing here is treated as having "already happened" in the main timeline — it's Pam's dream picking up on something true she half-noticed, same as Sofía's dream in *Office Noir 2* picked up on the blueprint.
- **The CV-in-an-envelope echoes, not contradicts, the Civil War plant** ("Sofía's CV is in a bodyguard's pocket," episodes 12/14). This is the same object, seen early and askew, through a dream — not a different, conflicting version of the prop.
- **Nothing is carried into the next numbered episode as "known."** Pam wakes with a private hunch and an envelope, not with proof she acts on. The series keeps deciding when she actually learns this for real.
- **Don't develop:** Pam-Cat is this episode's costume on an existing character, not a new recurring one.

## Risks I can see now

| Risk | Why | What I would do |
|---|---|---|
| Three narrated lines before anything happens (01–02) | A dream told mostly in voice-over risks failing the mute test, same risk flagged for *Office Noir 2* | Each line already has a silent action under it (the drop-in save, the unseen exit); test the board on cold viewers before any animation |
| The hit-and-run beat reads as genuinely violent, not comic | A car hitting a person is a hard image to keep light | Keep it cartoon-fast (three frames: headlights, impact, already sitting up), no sound of impact, her line lands before the viewer can worry |
| Too many new locations for one short (street, car, crossing, casino, bedroom) | Office Noir 1 was one room; this is five | Each location is one simple graphic shape (a lamp and a wall; a car window; a crossing; one booth and one spotlight; a bed) — reuse the office set's lighting kit (blinds, key light, vignette) across all of them for visual unity |
| Dad shown before episode 12 reads as a spoiler, not a flash-forward | Needs to clearly feel like dream-logic noticing something, not the real timeline | Keep him a shadow/voice until the casino booth, and never give him a line that only makes sense if he's already met the Client in real continuity |

## For you to decide before the script

1. **The name.** Pam-Cat or Kitty-Pam (your note) — or the title above can carry it instead and she stays unnamed on screen.
2. **The title.** One of the three above, or yours.
3. **Where Pam wakes.** Her own bed, or at her desk (keeps the "still at work, even asleep" joke going) — the concept above uses her bed.
4. **How much of the Client/Dad conversation is heard vs. only seen** — the concept above keeps it entirely silent (map, boxes, a bigger logo) so nothing is said that could later be contradicted by episode 12–13's actual dialogue.
5. **Where it sits in the calendar.** Outside the season's count, like *Office Noir 2* — special, not a numbered Act episode.
