# UptimeZ Inc. — Episode 10: *First Search* — premise v1

Status: **premise only**, not a concept or a script. Built on [`../STORYLINE.md`](../STORYLINE.md) v3 and Tomas's grill answers (17 Nov).

## Logline

The garage owner comes back for the pilot's first real search: one van's service history, needed today for a warranty claim. Don sells him the Premium tier brilliantly, dodges the only question that matters, and when the search returns 0 RESULTS he takes Sofía's advice to "present it as a clean result" literally: he has the zero turned green. The robot does exactly what was approved and emails the Client a 100% success report.

## The joke in one line

**A data problem gets a colour fix.** Everyone does their job correctly, and the customer leaves with less than nothing.

## Where we are in the story

- **From episode 9 (*The Onboarding*):**
  - The Client signed a **limited pilot** with one testable condition: find a van's repair history.
  - Don, thinking it was a contract page, **signed Sofía's CV**. She now has his signature and a seat at the table.
- **The world rule doing the work:** Find works, but **no customer data was ever connected**. The van's history isn't in there; it can't be found.
- **Leads into episode 11 (*Customer Success*):** the Client arrives furious, and Pam finds the record by phone.

**Dependency:** episode 9 isn't scripted yet. If it changes, the pilot condition and the signed CV must survive as written here.

## Situation and stakes

- **Where:** the UptimeZ meeting room, with the big screen, in the one office.
- **Why today:** a customer's van is up on a lift at one of the Client's garages. Without its service history, the warranty claim fails and the Client pays for the repair himself. He arrives holding the warranty form, marked **TODAY**.
- **What the Client is:** not an idiot. He came to test exactly what he was promised.

## Who wants what in this episode

| Who | Wants here | Gets | Costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DON** | The Client's admiration, with Sofía watching | A real sale: the Premium upgrade | The customer's trust, the moment the question goes unanswered |
| **SOFÍA** | To show the Client she's the one who actually gets things done | The floor, briefly | Her advice ("present it as a clean result") becomes Don's green zero. Her spin has hit the one line she won't cross |
| **THE CLIENT** | One van's history, today | Premium, a green zero and a success report | Money, a warranty claim and patience |

**The two desires that collide:** Don's need to win the room against Sofía's need to look competent. Her spin advice and his literal mind combine into the episode's mechanism.

## Story beats (premise level, not shots)

1. **The ask:** the Client arrives with the warranty form: one van, its history, today. He checks his watch.
2. **Don's win:** before any search runs, Don pitches Premium so well the Client actually buys it. The best touch is sincere: Premium includes "priority indexing, coming soon", the very thing that's missing, sold in good faith. Don's first instinct after the close is to check whether Sofía saw.
3. **The question:** "Is my data even in there?" Don answers with features, and believes every word. The Client notices: he checks his watch. Pam, in the background, doesn't move.
4. **The search:** van's repair history. **0 RESULTS**, in red.
5. **The spin:** Sofía, to the Client, smoothly: present it as a clean result. Don takes it literally: "Can you make the zero green?" Robot: "Interface change. Human approval required." Don: "Approved." The zero turns green.
6. **The stamp:** the robot reports *100% of approved interface changes completed.* Every word is true. Sofía's smile holds half a second too long; she knows she's lost the room.
7. **The doorway:** the Client leaves with the claim unresolved. Reports are within the robot's permissions, so his phone pings: the success report. He stops in the doorway and reads it.
8. **The loop:** back to the big screen. Frame 0 is the empty search bar; the last frame is the same screen with a green zero and the stamp. The image returns; the story has moved.

## What carries forward (into episode 11)

- The Client is now a **Premium** customer: he pays more for nothing.
- The pilot condition has **failed**, but the dashboard and the emailed report both say **success**.
- **Pam knows**, and has said nothing.

## Audit of the premise: risks to watch at the concept stage

| Risk | Why | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| Three payoffs (upsell, green zero, email) compete | Canon rule: one comic mechanism | The mechanism is presentation over substance. The upsell sets it up and the email delivers it, so keep the upsell short |
| Don's dodge reads as lying | Canon: he believes his pitch | He answers with features he genuinely loves; never a wink |
| The Client looks foolish buying Premium | Canon: not an idiot | Premium answers his real pain, records across ten garages. The trap is "coming soon" |
| Sofía crosses her line | Canon: she spins presentation, never facts | "A clean result" is spin; the green zero is Don's doing. She sees the gap and loses the room, and that's her beat |
| Pam gets lost | Two or three speakers per beat | One silent shot of her knowing, with a binder or phone list as the plant for episode 11 |
| The email beat feels like a second ending | Length, and the loop | It's the turn of the last beat: the Client stops in the doorway, then the loop |
| Too much text for phone size | Mute rule | Short screen text only: TODAY, 0 RESULTS, the green stamp, the email subject line |

**Length estimate:** 45–60 s with every beat; real timing comes from the voice takes.
