## Logline

The garage owner comes back to the pilot's first real search. A parts manufacturer wants to see his transmission design on Friday, and he signed up so UptimeZ would keep it safe. Don sells him the Premium tier brilliantly, dodges "Is my blueprint even in there?", and when the search returns 0 RESULTS he takes Sofía's advice to "present it as a clean result" literally: he has the zero turned green. The robot does exactly what was approved and emails the Client a 100% success report. The audience knows the blueprint is confetti in the shredder bin behind them.

## The joke in one line

**A presentation fix for something that no longer exists.** Everyone does their job correctly; the customer leaves with less than nothing, and only we know how much less.

## Where we are in the story

- **From episode 9 (*The Onboarding*):**
  - The Client signed up because he keeps losing his blueprint.
  - Don, watching Sofía, swept it into the shredder. Its strips became the confetti under the green ONBOARDING COMPLETE stamp.
  - One strip landed in the Client's palm. **He pocketed it without looking** and left happy.
  - The rest of the strips are still in the **shredder bin**.
- **The world rule underneath:** Find works, but no customer data was ever connected. Even if the blueprint had been scanned, it wouldn't be in there.
- **Leads into episode 11 (*Customer Success*):** the Client comes back furious, Pam finds the strips in the shredder bin and tapes the blueprint back together, and the robot says "The result was obtained by a human."

**Dependency:** episode 9 must end with the strip pocketed unread, the remaining strips in the shredder bin, and the bin visible in the meeting room. (This changes concept v4, where he recognised the strip.)

## Situation and stakes

- **Where:** the UptimeZ meeting room, with the big screen and the **shredder with its clear bin** at the edge of frame, in the same place as in episode 9.
- **Why now:** a parts manufacturer wants to see his transmission system on Friday. It's the biggest chance his ten garages have had.
- **What the Client is:** not an idiot. He signed up for one thing, to never lose this again, and came to check that it works.

**The two desires that collide:** Don's need to win the room against Sofía's need to look competent. Her spin and his literal mind make the episode's mechanism.

## Story beats (premise level, not shots)

1. **The ask:** the Client arrives, hopeful: Friday, the manufacturer, "find my blueprint". He checks his watch. In the corner, the shredder bin still holds blue strips. Only we notice.
2. **Don's win:** before any search runs, Don pitches Premium, with secure document storage, so well the Client actually buys it. He sells it in good faith. After the close, his first instinct is to check whether Sofía saw.
3. **The question:** "Is my blueprint even in there?" Don answers with features he believes in. The Client checks his watch. Pam looks at the screen and says nothing.
4. **The search:** "blueprint". **0 RESULTS**, in red, with the blue strips in the bin in the same frame.
5. **The spin:** Sofía, smoothly: present it as a clean result. Don takes it literally: "Can you make the zero green?" Robot: "Interface change. Human approval required." Don: "Approved." The zero turns green.
6. **The stamp:** the robot reports *100% of approved interface changes completed.* Every word is true. Sofía's smile holds half a second too long.
7. **The doorway:** the Client leaves without his blueprint. Sending reports is within the robot's permissions, so his phone pings with the success report. He stops in the doorway to read it, and his other hand goes into his pocket, past the strip he doesn't know he has.
8. **The loop:** the big screen again. Frame 0 is the empty search bar; the last frame is the same screen with a green zero and the stamp, the blue strips in the bin beside it.

## What carries forward (into episode 11)

- The Client is now a **Premium** customer: he pays more for nothing.
- Friday is coming. The pilot condition has **failed**, but the dashboard and his inbox both say **success**.
- The strips are still in the **shredder bin**, and one is in **his pocket**.

## Audit of the premise: risks for the concept stage

| Risk | Guard |
|---|---|
| The bin is missed, and the irony with it | The same shredder and clear bin as in episode 9, in the same place, kept in frame in beats 1, 4 and 8; the strips are the only blue in the room |
| Three payoffs (upsell, green zero, email) compete | One mechanism, presentation over substance: the upsell sets it up and the email delivers it, so keep the upsell short |
| Don's dodge reads as lying | He answers with features he genuinely loves; never a wink |
| The Client looks foolish buying Premium | Premium answers his real pain, keeping the blueprint safe; the trap is that it was already gone |
| Sofía crosses her line | "A clean result" is spin; the green zero is Don's |
| Two blueprint episodes in a row feel repetitive | 9 destroys, 10 denies, 11 repairs: each has a different joke |
| The pocket strip is too subtle | It's a seed, not a payoff: one hand movement, no close-up. It pays off in episode 11 or later |

**Length estimate:** 45–60 s with every beat; real timing comes from the voice takes.
