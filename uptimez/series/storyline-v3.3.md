**99.9% uptime. 0% findability.**

## In one line

**A company sells confidence.** Every employee knows a different part of the truth, and the company survives because nobody can afford to admit the whole of it.

## What the show is about

People who are exceptionally good at one thing, disastrously bad at another, and unable to tell the two apart.

**The principle:** everyone gets what they want for one moment, and that success makes someone else's situation worse.

## The world

- **UptimeZ Inc.** is a Gen Z-styled AI start-up with LinkedIn manners. The corporate branding is the **show's presentation style**, not a claim that the company films its own private disasters.
- **UptimeZ Find** is the product. The search itself works, but **no customer's data was ever connected or cleaned**, so it returns **0 RESULTS**. Pam finds things by phoning people. No script may fix Find by accident; fixing it would need the integration nobody approves.
- **One office:** the cafeteria, the meeting room, the door everyone bursts through.
- **The visual signature:** a big green success stamp beside an obviously disastrous result. **Every stamp is technically true.**

## The four leads

### DON, CEO: the salesman who believes his own pitch

| | |
|---|---|

| **Private lie** | *If I convinced them, I must have been right.* |
| **Fatal contradiction** | He can make strangers trust him but can't make the woman he wants respect him. Selling feels to him like proof that the product works |
| **Under pressure** | He invents a grander title or a grander explanation |

| **Line he won't cross** | He never knowingly lies. He believes it while he says it |
| **Rule** | He gets a real win before every major failure. After a sale, his first instinct is to see whether Sofía witnessed it, not to check whether the product can deliver |

### PAM, project manager: the woman whose competence traps her

| | |
|---|---|
| **Wants** | Don's love and recognition |
| **Private lie** | *One more problem solved, and he'll finally see me.* |
| **Fatal contradiction** | She can't resist fixing something, even when leaving it broken would help her |
| **Her small darkness** | **Strategic silence.** Sometimes she knows the answer and lets Don squirm first, and she enjoys knowing what Sofía doesn't |
| **Silent reaction** | She has already started fixing it |
| **Line she won't cross** | She never lets the customer down |

### THE ROBOT: right about the facts, wrong about the situation

| | |
|---|---|
| **Wants** | Its findings to lead to correct decisions |
| **Operating belief** | *If every step follows the rules, the outcome must be correct.* |
| **Fatal contradiction** | It makes things worse through flawless procedure |
| **Can do on its own** | Stamp, sort, print, serve, report, reorganize anything not restricted |
| **Needs human approval for** | Money, access, customer commitments, changes to products or systems |
| **Silent reaction** | It produces a report |
| **Line it won't cross** | It never misreports a fact. It may chase a meaningless metric or a narrow success label, but every statement is technically true |
| **Rule** | Each promotion gives it a bigger title, never the authority to fix the real problem. It never becomes human, rebellious or evil |

**Civil War: decided and open.**

- **Decided:** the catastrophe is the coffee machine, caused by Don's unread "Approved." and the $80 nobody paid, not by anyone's plan to kill; Don's "solution" stays vanity, never a murder plot. The Client is taken for dead in 17 and is alive. His condition for Father is Pam, not a data connection: Find never works in this arc. Every episode 60–90 s.
- **Open:** why the Client survived (to be planted before 17); the arc's ending (THE HUMAN ALGORITHM below is one candidate; Don losing the chair is another); whether Father stays after the arc.
- **Folded in:** The Demonstration (13) is replaced by The Offer; The Refund is not needed (the Client is a player in every episode); Permission to Succeed goes into 15, Renewal Day into 16.
- **Bodyguards** stay supporting cast, not new recurring characters with arcs.

**A candidate finale (not locked): THE HUMAN ALGORITHM.** The Client knowingly pays for Pam's work, not for UptimeZ Find. Don: "AI-powered customer intelligence." Robot: "No AI was involved." Don: "Approved." The revenue is real; the product claim is still false.

## Rules every episode keeps

1. **The image loops; the story advances.** The last frame returns to frame 0, but one power relationship has changed. Example: the same desk, a new number on Pam's phone.
2. **Dramatic irony, with the timing varied.** Sometimes we see Don's mistake first, sometimes Sofía knows something we don't, sometimes Pam's rescue brings a surprise.
3. **One comic mechanism, driven by two competing desires.**
4. **Status moves in every scene**, and everyone else gets two or three speakers per beat plus one silent reaction.
5. **The robot is technically true.** It never lies, and it makes things worse by procedure.
6. **It works on mute.** Each character has a silent signature:
   - Don straightens his vest;
   - Sofía checks who noticed;
   - Pam starts fixing it;
   - the robot prints a report;
   - Mom looks at Don;
   - the Client checks his watch.
7. **Length follows the story:** 40–90 s, every pause earned.
