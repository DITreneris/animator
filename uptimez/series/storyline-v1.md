# UptimeZ Inc. — the series storyline

**99.9% uptime. 0% findability.**

## In one line

A polished AI start-up sells a search product that finds nothing, run by a vain CEO who approves everything without looking. The only one who tells the truth is the office robot, and it can't act without a human's approval.

## The idea underneath

The AI is never the problem. It does exactly what it's told and says exactly what is true. The humans are the bug: they overrule it, flirt past it, or make it sign the form. Every episode puts the robot's honest verdict ("ACCURATE", "Height is not protected", "Inappropriate balls detected") against a human who approves the wrong thing anyway.

The brand is the straight man. Videos are posted as if from the company's own corporate account, and the people inside it are the joke.

## The world

- **UptimeZ Inc.** is a Gen Z-styled SaaS company with a pulse-line logo and LinkedIn manners.
- **UptimeZ Find** is its product, as *The Office* had paper: a search engine that always returns **"0 results"**. The company has almost no clients and doesn't notice.
- **One office:** desks, the meeting room, the cafeteria, a door everyone bursts through. Holidays happen there.

## Where it's heading

**The spine is the one client.** UptimeZ Find finds nothing, and the garage owner is the first person who really needs it to work.
- In *The Onboarding* he signs up.
- In a later episode he's unhappy, and the company has to keep him or die.

Every character plays that threat in character:
- Don approves the wrong fix.
- Sofía flirts with the client.
- Pam smooths things over.
- The robot tells the truth nobody wants.
- Mom shouts.

**The side thread is Father.** He was "parking" at the end of *The Best Candidate* because he's applying for Don's job. He's held back for when the client crisis gives him an opening.

## Rules every episode keeps

1. **It loops.** The last frame leads back into the first, and the ending re-starts the joke.
2. **The robot is right.** It is never the villain. It's stopped by needing human approval, or by a human hiding the verdict.
3. **Don never learns.** He approves, flirts, gives himself a title, and is saved or shrunk by someone else (usually a woman).
4. **Sofía wins quietly; Pam wins invisibly.** Sofía bounces Don every time. Pam's win is one look, one cuff, one kiss on a card.
5. **One running gag per character.** The ketchup stain (seen, rarely mentioned), "Approved.", "Pam?", the HR verdict stamp, "DONNIE!".
6. **It works on mute.** Verdicts are stamped on screen, and key lines are written as well as spoken.
7. **The holidays happen at the office.** Costumes go on the existing characters.
