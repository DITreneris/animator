## The idea in one line

A man with ten garages walks in and says "I'd like to sign up." It takes five people, three denied touches and seventy seconds to let him.

**The engine:** a simple task takes the whole office. **The fuel:** everyone is watching the wrong person.

## Who wants what

| Person | Shows | Hides |
|---|---|---|
| **The Client** | A polite, solid man who has decided to buy | Nothing. Ten garages, paperwork everywhere, and he cannot find any of it |
| **Don** | A CEO closing a deal | The deal does not matter. Sofía watching does |
| **Sofía** | Warm, efficient customer care | First she simply likes him. Then she hears "ten garages". By the end she is planning the wedding |
| **Pam** | Process and paperwork | Every form is a reason to stand beside Don |
| **HR robot** | Perfect compliance | Nothing. Hands may not touch; a card may |

## The running gag

Human contact is denied in red. Payment is approved in green. Same rule, three deliveries.

| # | Beat | Contact | How the robot does it | Line |

**Twin framing:** beats 02 and 07 use the same camera. Two hands with a robot between them, red. A card and a terminal, green. No line has to explain it.

**The rule:** objects may pass between people, hands may not. Sofía never touches the Client. She hands him the tablet, the pack.

## The smell and the thumbprint

- **No smell lines are drawn.** He is impressive, not dirty. The smell is acting only.
- **Don dislikes it** (beat 02). He leans in for the handshake and his nose wrinkles. The man with the ketchup stain looks down on a man who smells of work.
- **Sofía likes it** (beat 06). She leans in to show him where to tap and breathes in. Half a second, eyes closed.
- **The thumbprint** (beat 06). His tap leaves an oily print on the tablet. It is his signature, and an echo of Don's ketchup thumbprint in *Secret Santa*.

**Count:** 18 lines (Robot 5, Client 4, Don 4, Sofía 3, Pam 2), five texts to read.

**Three silences to protect:** after "0 results"; before "CONTACT APPROVED."; after "CONTACT DENIED."

**Three endings, one outcome:** the party is a false victory (short), the hand in the air is the defeat (long), the receipt is the product's punchline (quick).

## The Client: brief

- **Who:** owns ten garages across the city. Built them himself. Polite, patient, successful, and lost in his own paperwork.
- **Why he buys after "0 results":** his system also finds nothing, only slower.
- **How ten garages reads without a caption:** a heavy ring of keys on his belt that jingles when he moves, and the two words in his interrupted line.
- **Look:** a clean work jacket with his garages' patch, a rag in the back pocket, big hands with oil in the creases, a good watch. Solid, mid-forties. Nothing like Don (bald, vest), the macho (broad, handsome) or the small one (spiky, glasses).
- **His patience wears down:** friendly smile → polite confusion → tired acceptance → relief when Sofía helps → the final shrug. These five faces go on his sheet, plus the angry ones for the next episode.
- **Voice:** warm, low, unhurried. Three auditions chosen by measured pitch, away from Don, the macho and the small one.
- **Name:** nobody asks. His badge says VISITOR.

## Sofía: look and arc

- **Pearls:** drop earrings that swing (studs vanish at phone size), one strand over the dark collar, a bracelet. The bracelet is what identifies her hand in the "Tap here" and CV inserts.
- **Arc:** charmed → interested ("ten garages") → leaning in → walking him out without a glance at Don. She is never cruel to Don; she simply stops seeing him.
- **Not last episode again:** with the macho she was flustered by looks and did nothing. Here she is drawn to a man of substance who smells of work, and she acts.

## Canon to keep

- **Don:** fleece vest and lanyard, the ketchup stain there and unmentioned. He checks his look in his phone, not in glass.
- **Pam:** reading glasses on the chain. Professional throughout; her dignity is the joke. Her arc: hopeful → rejected → hopeful → rejected.
- **Robot:** the HR robot. No emotion, no hesitation, no apology, and no new functions.
- **The Client is not a fool.** He compares speed, and accepts the metric without question.

## How it meets our rules

| Rule | In this concept |

| One joke | A simple task takes everyone |
| Reads muted | One nameable action per beat (last column) |
| Text to read | Five inserts: the demo, the CV, the payment, the receipt, the end card. Robot rulings are a colour and a voice |
| One speaker per shot | Singles with clear wall for every line; group shots are for looks |
| Characters large | Five people never share one vertical frame |
| Close-ups | Used for a change of feeling, not only for a line |

## Length, counted

At the pace measured on *The Best Candidate* (1.45 s per shot, 1.36 s per line): 18 lines is about 25 s of speech, and about 48 shots is about 70 s. Four lines went out and about six reaction shots came in, so the total holds. The audit's 64 s is possible; I would plan for 70.

**First cuts if it runs long:** one look in beat 01, the umbrella in beat 08, "That's it?".

## Risks

- **Two crush episodes in a row.** The arc and the CV make this one different, but it is the same triangle.
- **The CV at phone size.** It needs its own insert, large, or Don's crumpled notes have no cause.
- **Pearls on a pale blouse.** They need the dark collar behind them and an outline.
- **Confetti and bubbles.** The bubble checks run on every drawing.
- **A new set** with a glass door, a demo desk and one monitor.
